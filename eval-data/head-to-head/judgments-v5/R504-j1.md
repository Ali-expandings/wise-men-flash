**B**

- It applies a 3:1 ratio on top of a 36-month-truncated LTV to get $1,000–1,500. That stacks conservatism without saying so.
- The Critic's survivorship mechanism ("young cohorts haven't had time to churn yet") is confused. "Dominated by cohorts that already shed their weak-fit customers" conflicts with a growing 3-year-old base, which skews toward new cohorts. "Probably worse than 1.2%" is overclaimed.

**C**
- "CAC payback kills the $5,000 number on its own" overstates. A long payback is a cash and risk constraint, not proof the spend loses money.
- "Every version of that is longer than the company has existed" is wrong for the 80%-margin simple payback, which is about 35 months.
- It treats agreement among models given identical inputs as evidence the number "isn't arbitrary."

**D**
- It calls revenue-vs-margin for the $180 "decisive and currently unknown" and makes confirming it the first step. The question states it is revenue, and D's own final section says so.
- The synthesis says survivorship bias "likely biases the estimate upward" and that a fresh cohort's "steady-state churn is plausibly worse" than 1.2%. Stickier survivors imply lower late-tenure churn, and a growing base skews young, so that direction is unsupported.
- "Extrapolating decades of future behavior" overstates an implied mean lifetime of about 7 years.
- In the same point, it calls the $5,300 figure both a "ceiling estimate" and "a floor."

- It calls the 36-month-truncated version "the most defensible" and says it puts customers "at or below breakeven." That treats zero value after month 36 as the best estimate rather than a floor.
