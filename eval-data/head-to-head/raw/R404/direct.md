**Weight to put on it: low. Treat 30% as a ceiling, not an expectation.**

It's one customer, hand-picked by the vendor to help close a large deal — that's survivorship bias, not a sample. You don't know if it's their best result or their median. No confounds are controlled: did that customer also change staffing, ship a product fix, or add self-serve docs the same quarter? "Ticket volume reduction" is also a squishy metric — deflection, faster resolution, and reclassification of what counts as a ticket all get lumped under the same headline number. And tools deliver the biggest gains at the least mature support orgs; if that customer had no KB/macros/chatbot before and you already do, their lever likely doesn't transfer to you. Rule of thumb: haircut vendor case-study numbers by half or more when building your business case — a case study is marketing collateral, not evidence.

**What would need to be true for it to apply to you:**

- **Distribution, not anecdote** — ask for results across their full customer base: median, range, and the share who saw little/no improvement or churned. If they can't or won't produce this, 30% is an outlier being sold as typical.
- **Comparability** — does that customer match you on ticket volume, ticket mix, industry, team size, and existing tooling maturity? A tool that crushes "reset my password" tickets is irrelevant if your volume is billing escalations that need a human anyway.
- **Methodology** — what exactly was measured, against what baseline, over what window, adjusted for seasonality/growth or not, and what else changed for that customer in the same period.
- **Your ticket taxonomy vs. their mechanism** — pull your own top ticket categories and check whether what the tool actually does (deflection, search, macros, AI triage) touches those categories, or just the easy long-tail that's a small share of your volume.
- **A real reference call, not the write-up** — talk to that customer directly: what they'd do differently, how long results took to show up, what they had to build or staff to get there. Better still, get a reference the vendor didn't pick — G2/Capterra reviews, or your own network.
- **A pilot on your own data before signing** — given the contract size, push for a time-boxed pilot or phased rollout measured against your actual ticket stream, plus contract terms that share the risk: milestone-based payment, service credits, or an exit clause if the promised reduction doesn't show up in your numbers.

Bottom line: build the ROI case on a conservative number — your pilot data, or half the claimed figure — not the vendor's best customer story, and structure the contract so that if 30% doesn't materialize, you're not the only one holding the risk.
