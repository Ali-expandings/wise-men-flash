## Council: Buying the Landscaping Business

**Architect:** Reasonable to lean toward buying the business itself — not yet reasonable to accept the financing as currently framed; those are two different questions.
Use the year before retirement to turn your two real unknowns — financial competence, client loyalty — from guesses into tested facts before you commit your capital.

**Skeptic:** The question conflates "is this a good business" with "is this an acceptable way to pay for it" — split them.
Insider succession is one of the best acquisition setups there is; don't reject the business over financing terms that are still negotiable.

**Pragmatist:** Lean yes, but treat the year as mandatory diligence, not a waiting room — take over bidding and shadow the cash cycle for one full season before signing.
You have real leverage right now, because the boss needs a buyer before the clock runs out — use it to push for seller financing or an earn-out instead of funding this alone.

**Critic:** As currently structured — full price, all cash, all your leverage, no live test — walk away.
Fixed monthly debt service meeting unverified, possibly boss-specific revenue in a bad season is exactly how this fails, and you'd have no cushion left when it happens.

### Verdict
- **Consensus:** All four voices rate the underlying opportunity unusually good (insider succession, 8 years of operational trust, a full year of runway) and independently converge on the same fix: don't sign until you've spent this year actually running bidding/books yourself and fielding client renewals directly, and don't finance it as pure savings-plus-bank-loan if the boss can be talked into carrying a note or earn-out instead.
- **Strongest dissent:** Skeptic and Critic both headline "walk away" (from the deal as currently offered); Pragmatist and Architect headline "yes, conditionally." The gap is framing more than substance — but Critic's warning matters: a soft "conditional yes" can quietly become "waited a year, learned nothing new, bought blind anyway" if the conditions aren't enforced as hard gates, not good intentions.
- **Premise check:** Yes — Skeptic rejected the question's binary framing outright, arguing this is really two stapled-together decisions (business quality vs. financing structure), and that bundling them makes a good target look like a bad decision.
- **Recommendation:** Your leaning is reasonable as a direction, premature as a decision. Spend the year converting unknowns into evidence — run estimating/bidding yourself this season with the boss auditing, become the point of contact for renewals so you see real retention data, see the actual books monthly. Walk away if: the boss won't open the books or hand you bidding/client contact before closing; a meaningful slice of clients turn out to be loyal to him personally once you're the contact; he refuses any seller financing, earn-out, or paid transition period and insists on 100% cash at close; your own cash-flow model, once you've seen real numbers, can't survive one bad season alongside full debt service; or your partner gets more nervous, not less, once the real numbers are on the table.
