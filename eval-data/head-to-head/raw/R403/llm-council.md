## Council Verdict: Setting the Onboarding Step-3 Drop-Off Target

### Where the Council Agrees
- **Sequencing is backwards.** A number is being demanded before any diagnosis of *why* users leave at step three. Even the one advisor arguing for committing a number now concedes diagnosis might reveal something quite different.
- **"Cut it in half" is arithmetic, not evidence.** Half of 40 is 20 regardless of what's actually causing the 40 — it reads as a slide-ready anchor, not a number derived from cohort data or failure-mode analysis.
- **The step-3 rate is a proxy, not the real target.** What should matter is downstream activation, completed signups, or retention — not the step-3 completion rate in isolation. Near-unanimous, including from the one dissenting advisor.
- **The diagnostic method is broadly agreed:** segment the funnel by device/channel/cohort, watch session replays, run exit interviews, and pinpoint the exact failure moment inside step three before doing anything else.
- **The framing itself is the problem, not just the number.** A quarter allocated, a goal set, and a number demanded — all before any diagnostic work exists — is what deserves the pushback, not just the specific target.

### Where the Council Clashes
**Commit a number now vs. commit nothing yet.** One advisor breaks from the rest, proposing 20% as a floor with a one-way "renegotiate up" clause if diagnosis finds more is possible. The other four say: hand leadership a range or confidence level, not a point number, until diagnosis lands. Peer review sided against the floor proposal — but its underlying worry is real: leadership explicitly asked for a number, and pure delay with nothing to hold may not survive that conversation.

**Is less step-3 drop-off even good?** Two advisors raise self-selection: some of that 40% may be people correctly filtering themselves out — pushing them deeper could hurt downstream retention rather than help. The dissenting advisor treats all step-3 friction as bad and fixable, never engaging this possibility. This is a genuine, unresolved fork: the council debates "is 20% achievable" far more than it debates "is 20% even the right thing to want."

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
- **Nobody designed for attribution.** Every peer review independently flagged this: no advisor proposed how to prove, at quarter's end, that any movement was caused by the redesign versus seasonality, novelty effects, channel-mix shifts, or noise. No holdout, no A/B, no check on whether the 40% baseline itself is even stable. Hit 20% in twelve weeks and it proves nothing without this.
- **The lone dissenting advisor contradicts itself** — arguing against premature number-setting, then setting a premature number. Reviewers caught this independently and consistently. It's also the only advisor to skip the self-selection risk entirely, which makes it the riskier position: pushing for a *more* aggressive target on an unexamined assumption.
- **Nobody planned for leadership simply saying no** to a diagnostic delay, and nobody built in a mechanism to revisit the target mid-quarter as real data arrives in either direction.

### The Recommendation
Don't hand leadership 20%. Don't hand them a bare "we need more time" either — hand them a counter-proposal with a date attached.

Ask for two weeks, ring-fenced, diagnosis-only, carved out of the quarter, before any target is named or design file opened. In that window: segment the funnel by device/channel/new-vs-returning, pinpoint the exact failure point inside step three, watch 10-15 replays of real drop-offs, run 5 exit interviews. In parallel, force whoever owns activation/retention to state in writing — before any number exists — what a 20-point step-3 gain is actually supposed to buy downstream. No answer means the team is about to spend a quarter chasing a vanity metric.

That diagnostic resolves three things at once: which mechanism dominates (fixable friction vs. healthy self-selection — this decides whether lowering the number is even desirable), what number is realistic (one dominant blocker means 50% may be conservative; five scattered causes means 50% is fiction), and what the real scoreboard should be (downstream activation/retention, with step-3 rate demoted to a leading indicator). Build the measurement plan — a holdout or A/B, not before/after eyeballing — alongside it, so whatever number ships at quarter's end can actually be attributed to the work. If leadership needs something to hold today, give them a range, explicitly tied to "a real number, with reasoning, in two weeks" — not a floor, and not silence.

### The One Thing to Do First
Before opening any design file or naming any number, take the two-week, ring-fenced diagnostic counter-proposal to leadership. The target gets set at the end of it, not before.
