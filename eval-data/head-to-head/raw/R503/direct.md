Before picking a lane, pressure-test the "7 of 10" number. Sales-logged loss reasons skew toward whatever's easiest to blame — "missing feature" is a lot more comfortable to write up than "we got outsold" or "we were priced wrong" or "we pitched the wrong buyer." Pull 3-4 of those deals and check with the actual prospect or the AE's notes/call recordings whether automation was really the deciding factor or just the visible excuse. If it holds up, keep reading.

**The real choice isn't binary.** Build vs. buy skips a third option that fits your situation better: license or embed an existing automation engine (Workato, Tray.ai, n8n, Zapier's partner/embed API) into your product. That gets a genuinely polished builder in front of prospects in weeks, costs a subscription or rev-share instead of your cash reserve, and buys you real data on whether automation actually closes deals before you commit two quarters or your war chest to it.

**Why "buy" is the riskier of your two named options.** At 80 people, your cash reserve isn't spare change — it's your runway through a bad quarter, a slow raise, a downturn. Spending most of it to acquire a small startup for one feature is a bet-the-company move, and small-company acquisitions almost never save the time you think they will: replatforming the acquired product onto your auth/billing/data model, retaining the founding team past their cliff, merging support and docs — that's typically 12-18 months, not zero. You don't skip the two quarters, you add integration risk and cash risk on top of a similar timeline. It only makes sense if you're already profitable (reserve isn't your survival buffer), the price is small relative to reserve rather than "most of it," or the team's expertise is the real prize and the product is secondary.

**Why "build in two quarters" is optimistic.** The core workflow engine might land in two quarters. The polish — templates, edge-case handling, reliability, the UX refinement that's actually what "polished" means to a buyer comparing you to a competitor who's had a head start — usually runs 2-3x that. And pulling meaningful engineering headcount off roadmap for two-plus quarters at an 80-person company has real opportunity cost elsewhere; account for what else slips.

**Recommendation:** don't spend most of your cash reserve on this. Stop the bleeding now with a partner/embed integration, build the differentiated core in-house on a realistic (not optimistic) timeline, and keep the acquisition option only for a target priced well under "most of reserve" or where you're clearly buying a team, not a feature. If you're already cash-flow positive and the reserve isn't your lifeline, or no embeddable option fits your stack (heavily regulated, on-prem, etc.), that changes the math toward buy — but as framed, spending your buffer to fix one feature gap is disproportionate to the risk.
