**Final Recommendation**

Tone is neither a plain technical fix nor a personal apology — it's a third register: factual ownership. State what was wrong, state what's correct now, one direct sentence of responsibility, then move to impact and next steps. Technical-only reads as hiding something behind small words ("methodology update"). Apology-only makes the memo about the writer's guilt and forces executives to manage feelings on top of a correction.

Address the counterfactual, briefly. Give the corrected number and its direct arithmetic effect — losses were $X, not $Y. Don't render the verdict yourself. You likely don't own every input into that call (lease, headcount, strategy); stating the corrected fact and letting executives judge its weight is more honest and more defensible than arguing a conclusion for them. Silence on this doesn't dodge the question — it guarantees someone asks it back to you later, in a worse setting.

Include one forward-looking action: check whether the same formula touched any other regional analysis, and say you're doing it. This is the highest-leverage line in the message.

Loop your manager in before executives, even two lines of notice. Cheap insurance against blindsiding your own chain of command on something this size.

How these usually go wrong:

- Over-apologizing: long, emotional apologies center the writer, not the fix; read as pre-managing blame rather than delivering facts.
- Under-owning: bloodless technical language that buries the magnitude reads as evasive — worse than owning it if surfaced later by someone else.
- Arguing the counterfactual too hard in either direction — both read as self-serving.
- No proposed next step — leaves the reader wondering if this is isolated or systemic, and whether you've thought past the apology.
- Vagueness on discovery date — invites "did you know sooner?"
- Implying the closure should be reversed when that's not realistic — sets up a deflating non-outcome, reads as naive about what's actually recoverable now.

Keep it short. State it, own it, size it, fix the process, stop.
