## Council: Tone for the office-closure correction

**Architect:** Lead with the precise correction — what was wrong, the right numbers — plus one direct line owning it; keep speculation about the decision's outcome out of the memo itself.
Executives need accurate numbers on record regardless of tone, and guessing at a counterfactual you can't verify invites either defensiveness or overcorrection.

**Skeptic:** "Tone" is the wrong first question — this is an analytical and organizational problem wearing a tone costume; bound the "would it have changed the call" question with real numbers, and don't send this solo.
The three tones aren't alternatives to pick between — they're ingredients (fact, proportionate ownership, forward action) that a pick-one framing leaves out.

**Pragmatist:** Lead with facts and the corrected number, add one genuine, non-boilerplate sentence on the human stakes, and send it today after sweeping other reports for the same formula error.
Speed and a demonstrated audit rebuild trust faster than a perfectly calibrated paragraph ever will.

**Critic:** Same shape — precise correction, plain ownership, no speculation — but route it through a manager and likely Legal/HR before it reaches the exec team, and never put a "might not have justified closure" line in writing.
A memo revising the record behind a completed layoff is discoverable; unreviewed causal speculation is exactly what turns an honest correction into liability.
