## Recommendation

Stay on Postgres. Add a BM25-capable full-text extension (ParadeDB `pg_search`, or your provider's equivalent) on top of `tsvector`/GIN rather than standing up a dedicated search cluster. All three council members reached this conclusion independently.

## Why

- **4 million documents isn't actually a scale problem.** All three members placed it well inside Postgres full-text search's comfortable range (estimates spanned "tens of millions" to "10–50M+ on one well-provisioned node") — for reference, English Wikipedia (~6–7M articles) and Stack Overflow (~20M+ questions) both run bigger corpora on this kind of setup. "Dedicated search cluster" also overstates what 4M documents actually requires: roughly 1–3 shards, effectively a single node plus a replica.
- **The "ranking gets bad past a certain size" claim is real but mis-stated.** Postgres's `ts_rank`/`ts_rank_cd` has no IDF term and no length normalization by default, so it loses to BM25 on ranking quality — but that gap exists at any corpus size, it just gets more visible as more documents match a query. The sharper version of the problem: ranked top-N queries can't be answered from a GIN index alone (GIN/GiST decide what matches, not the order), so Postgres must fetch and score every matching row before returning the top 10 — cost scales with how many rows match a given query, not with total corpus size. One benchmark cited by the architecture reviewer showed a low-selectivity query going from 37s to ~300ms once a BM25 index could push the top-K ordering down into the index itself. That's the number worth chasing, not "4 million" as a round threshold.
- **A dedicated cluster relocates the team's risk, it doesn't remove it.** All three members agreed self-managed Elasticsearch/OpenSearch is the wrong fit for five engineers with no cluster experience — shard sizing, JVM/heap tuning, snapshot/restore, and version upgrades are a real multi-month learning curve with a good chance of a multi-day incident in year one. Managed hosting (Elastic Cloud, OpenSearch Service) removes the infrastructure-babysitting half of that but not the mapping/analyzer/relevance-tuning half; only fully hosted search-as-a-service (Typesense Cloud, Meilisearch Cloud) meaningfully removes "running a cluster" as a concept, and even then you still own the sync pipeline.
- **"A second copy that can drift" is solvable, known engineering — but it's work you'd be taking on for a problem Postgres doesn't have.** The council converged on a transactional outbox for near-real-time sync (safer than dual writes, lighter than Kafka/CDC for a team this size), a routine tested full-reindex-with-alias-swap as the reconciliation backstop, and treating any external index as a non-authoritative pointer rather than a second source of truth.

## Tradeoffs and risks

- Verify extension availability on your specific Postgres host before committing to this path. `pg_search`/ParadeDB is confirmed unavailable on stock RDS/Aurora, and one council member's live check found Neon deprecated `pg_search` for existing projects effective 2026-09-21 — days from now. Confirm current status with your provider directly rather than trusting this memo on that point; if unsupported, fall back to RUM indexes or well-tuned `ts_rank_cd` with field weighting (`setweight`), which closes much of the gap for free with no new extension.
- Add the `tsvector` column carefully. A naive `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN tsv tsvector GENERATED ALWAYS AS (...) STORED` on a live 4M-row table takes an ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock and rewrites the whole table. Backfill in batches on a nullable column, then build the index with `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`.
- If you ever add an external index later, treat it as a non-authoritative cache, not a second source of truth: re-fetch canonical rows and re-check permissions from Postgres before returning results. A stale ACL in a search index is a data leak, not a relevance bug.
- If logical replication/CDC ever becomes your sync mechanism, know that a stalled consumer can let the replication slot retain WAL indefinitely and fill your primary database's disk — a search-sync failure that can take down the actual database. The transactional outbox pattern doesn't carry this failure mode and is the better default for a team this size.
- If search load ever contends with OLTP performance, move search queries to a physical read replica before reaching for a second system — same data, same consistency guarantees, zero drift risk.
- Weight the reports by evidence quality, not 3-for-3 agreement: Opus and Sonnet did their own current-state research and produced sharper, more falsifiable claims (including the two provider-specific findings above); Haiku, standing in for an unavailable open-source model, mostly corroborated them rather than successfully breaking the consensus, and its figures were rounder and unverified. Treat specific numbers across all three reports (scaling thresholds, latency, managed-service pricing) as directional, not measured against your actual workload.

## Final call

Benchmark before building: spend 3–5 days running a judged query set (100–200 real or representative queries) against a copy of your actual data to measure ranking quality and p95 latency on plain `ts_rank`. That turns "I'm told relevance gets bad" into your own numbers. Then ship `tsvector` + GIN with field-weighted (`setweight`) scoring first — days of work, no new dependency — and add a BM25 extension only if the benchmark shows the ranking gap actually matters for your queries, after confirming your host supports one. Build sync and reindex tooling only if and when you later decide to add an external index. Pre-commit to concrete revisit triggers now — corpus past roughly 25–50M documents, p95 latency that doesn't recover even with a BM25 index, or hard requirements (faceting, typo tolerance, multi-language, non-engineer-editable synonyms) Postgres genuinely can't cover — so a future move is driven by data, not folklore.
