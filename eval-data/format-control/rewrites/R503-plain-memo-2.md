## Why

**The "7 of 10" number needs a test first.** Sales-logged loss reasons skew toward whatever is easiest to blame. "Missing feature" is a lot more comfortable to write up than "we got outsold," "we were priced wrong," or "we pitched the wrong buyer." So the figure should be pressure-tested before it drives a decision of this size.

**A third option beats the binary.** You can license or embed an existing automation engine in your product, such as Workato, Tray.ai, n8n or Zapier's partner/embed API. That gets a genuinely polished builder in front of prospects in weeks. It costs a subscription or rev-share instead of your cash reserve. It also buys you real data on whether automation actually closes deals, before you commit two quarters or your war chest to it.

**Buying is the riskier of your two named options.** At 80 people, your cash reserve is not spare change. It is your runway through a bad quarter, a slow raise, or a downturn. Spending most of it to acquire a small startup for one feature is a bet-the-company move. Small-company acquisitions almost never save the time you think they will. Replatforming the acquired product onto your auth, billing and data model takes work. So does retaining the founding team past their cliff, and so does merging support and docs. Together that is typically 12-18 months, not zero. So you do not skip the two quarters. You add integration risk and cash risk on top of a similar timeline.

Buying only makes sense in three cases. The first is that you are already profitable, so the reserve is not your survival buffer. The second is that the price is small relative to the reserve rather than "most of it". The third is that the team's expertise is the real prize and the product is secondary.

**Building in two quarters is optimistic.** The core workflow engine might land in two quarters. The polish usually runs 2-3x that. Polish here means templates, edge-case handling, reliability, and the UX refinement that is actually what "polished" means to a buyer comparing you to a competitor who has had a head start. Pulling meaningful engineering headcount off the roadmap for two-plus quarters at an 80-person company also has real opportunity cost elsewhere, so you need to account for what else slips.
