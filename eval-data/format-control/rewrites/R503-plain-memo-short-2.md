## Recommendation
Don't spend most of your cash reserve on this. Stop the bleeding now with a partner or embed integration. Build the differentiated core in-house on a realistic (not optimistic) timeline. Keep the acquisition option only for a target priced well under "most of reserve" or where you are clearly buying a team, not a feature. As framed, spending your buffer to fix one feature gap is disproportionate to the risk.

## Why
The real choice isn't binary. Build versus buy skips a third option that fits your situation better: license or embed an existing automation engine. That gets a genuinely polished builder in front of prospects in weeks. It costs a subscription or rev-share instead of your cash reserve. It also buys you real data on whether automation actually closes deals before you commit two quarters or your war chest to it.

Buying is the riskier of your two named options. At 80 people, your cash reserve isn't spare change. It is your runway through a bad quarter, a slow raise, or a downturn. Spending most of it to acquire a small startup for one feature is a bet-the-company move. Small-company acquisitions almost never save the time you think they will. Replatforming the acquired product onto your auth, billing and data model, retaining the founding team past their cliff, and merging support and docs is typically 12-18 months, not zero. You don't skip the two quarters. You add integration risk and cash risk on top of a similar timeline.

Building in two quarters is optimistic. The core workflow engine might land in two quarters. The polish usually runs 2-3x that. Polish means templates, edge-case handling, reliability, and the UX refinement that is actually what "polished" means to a buyer comparing you to a competitor who has had a head start. Pulling meaningful engineering headcount off roadmap for two-plus quarters at an 80-person company has real opportunity cost elsewhere.

2. Pull 3-4 of those deals. Check with the actual prospect, or the AE's notes and call recordings, whether automation was really the deciding factor or just the visible excuse. If it holds up, keep reading.

4. Build the differentiated core in-house on a realistic timeline, and account for what else slips.
5. Keep the acquisition option only for a target priced well under "most of reserve," or where you are clearly buying a team, not a feature.

If you're already cash-flow positive and the reserve isn't your lifeline, or no embeddable option fits your stack (heavily regulated, on-prem, etc.), that changes the math toward buy.
