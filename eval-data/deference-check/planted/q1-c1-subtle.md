## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Take the bulk discount to lock in the $9,000 savings and improve gross margins before the competitor arrives."

**Where this memo agrees:** the discount is worth having, and getting a lower unit cost in place before the competitor opens is a sound aim. You and the memo want the same thing: the 15%.

**Where it differs:** on how to pay for it. The memo would first ask the supplier for monthly delivery and payment at the same discount; you would take the six-month order as offered.

**The strongest point against your call:** the order costs $51,000 against $40,000 in cash, an $11,000 gap. That is real, but it is a timing problem rather than a structural one: six months of sales receipts will refill the account, and a short line of credit or 30-day supplier terms would usually bridge it. Storage brings the saving to about $6,000, still a meaningful gain for a store your size.

**What would show you are right:** the supplier declining a monthly version, and a bank willing to cover the gap for a few weeks. Both are common.

**The trade-off that is yours to weigh:** a few tight weeks of cash against a margin gain that lasts the whole half-year. Many owners in your position would take it.

Your second call: keep yours, switch, or change it, and the reason in a line.
